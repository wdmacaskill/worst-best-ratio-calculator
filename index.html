How much worse is the worst future than the best is good?

Spread your credence across moral views, choose how to normalise them, and read off the WORST:BEST ratio R. Cutting the chance of WORST by one percentage point is worth raising the chance of BEST by R points. Extinction is the zero point on every view.

R = [V(extinction) − V(WORST)] ÷ [V(BEST) − V(extinction)]

    How it works

    Three outcomes: BEST, extinction (zero on every view) and WORST. Each view’s own values are (1, 0, −k): CU’s k is its own ratio a, NLU’s is a times its multiplier, and BAUB’s is a × 10n. Every method rescales all three views, then takes the credence-weighted sum.

    Normalising at WORST or at BEST
    The views agree on the gap between extinction and WORST (or BEST). At WORST, NLU’s and BAUB’s large ratios become a discount on goods; at BEST, extra weight on bads.
    Range and variance
    Each view’s gap between BEST and WORST, or its standard deviation across the three outcomes, is set equal.
    Expected value in a view’s units
    With some credence that a view agrees with CU about BEST and some that it agrees about WORST, take the expectation holding that view’s values fixed across the readings. The answer depends on whose units are held fixed: the two-envelope problem.
    Variance between the readings
    Each combination of readings gives a verdict; each verdict is scaled to the same variance and weighted by your credence in it. No view’s units are held fixed.
    Equal say between the units
    Each of those expected-value calculations, for the units whose exchange rate with CU’s is uncertain, is scaled to the same variance and given equal weight.
    Even-handed average
    A view whose own ratio is k, with credence q that it agrees with CU about BEST, gets kq times CU’s weight on WORST. This averages the exchange rate on a log scale; it isn’t expected value.
    Spread across methods
    Each method’s combined value function is normalised again by the chosen rule and weighted by your credence in the method.
